The GST billing software features that actually matter for a retail store are the ones that keep the counter moving and your returns accurate: fast barcode billing, correct GST calculation with HSN codes, e-invoicing where applicable, real-time inventory, offline mode, and clean GSTR reports your accountant can use directly. Loyalty programmes, dashboards and integrations are useful, but only once the basics work reliably during a Saturday evening rush. Below are the twelve features worth checking before you choose a billing and POS system, along with typical costs and questions to ask vendors.

Billing and GST compliance

1. Accurate GST calculation with HSN and SAC codes

The software must handle CGST, SGST and IGST correctly based on place of supply, support multiple tax slabs on one bill, and store HSN or SAC codes against each item. Check that it handles tax-inclusive and tax-exclusive pricing, since many retailers display MRP inclusive of tax.

2. E-invoicing and e-way bills

If your business crosses the turnover threshold for e-invoicing, the software should generate IRN and QR codes through the Invoice Registration Portal without manual uploads. For goods movement above the prescribed value, built-in e-way bill generation saves time. Thresholds change, so confirm current rules with your CA.

3. GSTR-ready reports

Look for GSTR-1 and GSTR-3B summaries, HSN-wise summaries and B2B versus B2C splits that can be exported in a format your accountant or the GST portal accepts. Poor reports mean hours of reconciliation at month end.

Speed at the counter

4. Fast barcode and keyboard billing

A cashier should be able to scan, change quantity, apply a discount and take payment without touching the mouse. Test this yourself with a real bill of fifteen items. Seconds per bill add up to long queues.

5. Offline mode

Internet outages happen, especially in markets and malls with patchy connectivity. The system should keep billing offline and sync automatically when the connection returns, without duplicate invoice numbers.

6. Multiple payment modes and split payments

Cash, UPI, cards and wallets on the same bill, with UPI QR codes shown on screen or printed on the receipt, and end-of-day reconciliation by payment mode.

7. WhatsApp and SMS receipts

Digital receipts cut paper costs, give customers a record they will not lose, and collect phone numbers you can use for loyalty and repeat purchase messages, with the customer's consent.

Inventory and business control

8. Real-time inventory with batch and expiry

Stock should update with every sale, return and purchase entry. Pharmacies, FMCG and cosmetics stores need batch and expiry tracking; apparel needs size and colour variants. Low-stock alerts prevent lost sales.

9. Purchase entries and supplier management

Recording purchase bills with input tax lets you track margins and claim input tax credit correctly. Supplier-wise purchase history also helps you negotiate.

10. User roles and audit trail

Cashiers should not be able to delete bills or change prices without permission. A log of edits, voids and discounts is one of the simplest ways to reduce leakage.

Features for growth

11. Multi-store and central reporting

If you plan a second outlet, pick software that supports multiple stores with central item masters, stock transfers and consolidated reports. Migrating later is painful.

12. Integrations

Useful connections include accounting software such as Tally, your online store, payment terminals and weighing scales. Ask for a list of existing integrations rather than promises of future ones.

What billing software typically costs

Pricing in the Indian market usually follows one of three models. Treat these as typical ranges only:

  • Desktop licence: a one-time fee often between ₹5,000 and ₹30,000 per system, with annual support or update charges.
  • Cloud subscription: commonly ₹300 to ₹2,000 per store per month, depending on features and users.
  • Free or freemium apps: fine for very small stores, but check limits on invoices, users and reports.

Also budget for hardware: a thermal printer, barcode scanner and, if needed, a cash drawer and label printer. A basic counter setup often costs ₹8,000 to ₹25,000 excluding the computer or tablet.

Key takeaway

Choose billing software on how it performs during your busiest hour and how clean its GST reports are at month end. Test offline billing, speed with a real basket and report exports before you commit, then look at loyalty and integrations.

Getting the setup right

Most billing problems after go-live come from a rushed setup, not from the software. Before the first bill, clean your item master: one name per product, correct HSN codes and tax rates, barcodes and opening stock counted physically. Train every cashier on returns, exchanges and voids, not just normal sales. Run the new system in parallel with the old one for a few days if you can, and compare the day-end totals.

How to evaluate before buying

  1. Ask for a trial with your own item list, not the vendor's demo data.
  2. Bill twenty real transactions at peak speed, including a return and a split payment.
  3. Pull a GSTR-1 export and send it to your accountant for a sanity check.
  4. Unplug the internet mid-bill and confirm what happens.
  5. Ask how data is backed up and how you can export everything if you leave.

Frequently asked questions

Is GST billing software mandatory for retailers?

GST law requires proper tax invoices, not a particular software. However, software makes compliance far easier, and businesses covered by e-invoicing rules effectively need a system that connects to the invoice registration portal.

Can I use billing software on a tablet or phone?

Many cloud POS systems run on Android tablets with Bluetooth printers. This works well for small stores and cafés; high-volume counters usually still prefer a desktop with a wired scanner.

What happens to my data if I switch software?

Ask the vendor before buying whether you can export item masters, customer lists and invoice history in standard formats such as Excel or CSV. Without this, switching becomes expensive.
