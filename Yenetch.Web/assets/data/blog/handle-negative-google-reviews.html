The right way to handle a negative Google review is to respond publicly within a day or two, calmly and without arguing, acknowledge the issue, take the details offline, and then actually fix the problem. Future customers read your reply as much as the complaint, so a professional response often does more for your reputation than the bad review does harm. Reviews that break Google's policies, such as fake reviews or abuse, can be flagged for removal, but genuine criticism stays. Over time the best defence is a steady flow of honest reviews from happy customers, so a single complaint does not define your listing.

What to do in the first 24 hours

  1. Do not reply in anger. Read the review, step away, and reply once you are calm.
  2. Check the facts. Find the order, appointment or bill. Talk to the staff involved.
  3. Decide the category using the escalation rules below.
  4. Reply publicly with a short, professional response.
  5. Contact the customer privately if you can identify them, to resolve the issue.

Escalation rules: which reviews need what

Not every negative review needs the same treatment. A simple set of rules helps your team respond consistently.

Response templates you can adapt

Templates save time, but always personalise them. A reply that obviously ignores what the customer said looks worse than no reply.

For a genuine service complaint

"Hello [Name], thank you for telling us about this, and we are sorry your visit on [day] did not go as it should have. [Specific acknowledgement, for example: a 40-minute wait is not acceptable.] We have shared this with our team and would like to make it right. Please call us on [number] or message us on WhatsApp, and ask for [manager's name]."

For a product issue

"Hi [Name], we are sorry the [product] did not meet your expectations. This is not the standard we aim for. Please share your order number at [email or WhatsApp] and we will arrange a replacement or refund as per our policy."

For a review you cannot trace

"Hello, we take all feedback seriously but cannot find a record of your visit or order. Please contact us at [number] with details so we can look into this properly."

What to avoid in any reply

  • Arguing about facts in public, even when you are right.
  • Sharing the customer's personal or medical details, or their order history.
  • Copy-pasting the same reply to every review.
  • Offering refunds or discounts in exchange for deleting the review.
  • Blaming the customer, a staff member by name or a supplier.

When a review can be removed

Google removes reviews that violate its policies: spam and fake content, off-topic posts, offensive language, conflicts of interest such as competitor or former-employee reviews, and personal information. You can flag such a review from your Business Profile and track the request's status. Removal is not guaranteed and can take time. Genuine negative experiences, however unfair they feel, generally stay. Be wary of anyone promising to remove honest reviews for a fee.

Key takeaway

Reply to every negative review within a day or two, calmly and specifically, and move the conversation offline. Flag only reviews that break Google's policies. The long-term fix is fixing the underlying problem and earning a steady stream of genuine reviews.

How to earn more genuine reviews

A listing with many recent, detailed reviews absorbs an occasional complaint easily. Building that base is a process, not a campaign.

  • Ask at the moment of satisfaction: after a successful treatment, a smooth delivery or when a customer thanks your staff.
  • Make it one tap: share a short review link on WhatsApp after the sale and put a QR code at the counter and on invoices.
  • Ask everyone, not just fans: Google's guidelines discourage selectively asking only happy customers, and a natural mix looks more credible anyway.
  • Do not offer incentives such as discounts or gifts for reviews; this breaks Google's policies.
  • Thank reviewers so customers see that feedback is read.
  • Train staff to mention reviews naturally, and give them a simple script.

Use reviews as operational feedback

Once a month, read all reviews from the last thirty days and tag the complaints: wait time, staff behaviour, product quality, billing, delivery. Recurring themes point to process problems that no reply can fix. Sharing positive reviews that name staff members also helps morale and reinforces good service.

Frequently asked questions

Can I delete a negative Google review myself?

No. Business owners cannot delete reviews. You can reply to them and flag those that violate Google's policies for review by Google.

Should I ask the customer to update their review after resolving the issue?

You can politely let them know the issue is resolved and that they are welcome to update their review if they wish. Do not make any remedy conditional on changing it.

How quickly should I respond to a negative review?

Within 24 to 48 hours is a good standard. A quick, calm reply shows future customers that you take problems seriously.

Do replies to reviews help local SEO?

Replies signal an active, engaged business and improve how customers perceive you. Their direct ranking effect is modest, but the reviews themselves and their frequency matter for local visibility.
